We try to hold a [HackWimbledon meetup](/meetups) every couple of weeks; we had one yesterday, we have another one planned for later in August, and then two in September. Check on [Luma](https://luma.com/hackwimbledon) for the upcoming dates.

So... if you have never been along to HackWimbledon before, what should you know in advance? Well, we've put a few things to know on our [About page](/about/#how-it-works), but here are a few more notes about what to expect.

HackWimbledon is a "pop-up hackspace" - we have a regular home at [Wimbletech](https://wimbletech.com), but we don't have a permanent workshop spacnge with a fixed set of equipment. Sometimes, we may have a spare Raspberry Pi or loaner laptop, but generally speaking, folks should bring their own kit and project to work on.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
You're really going to want to bring _something_ (a laptop, physical project, electronics kit etc) with you - it will be a lot less interesting otherwise!
{{< /callout >}}

Members of the group are interested in a range of different maker topics, including electronics, coding, robotics, 3D printing, physical electronic games, home automation, retro computing, and more. If you've got a project you're working on and are looking for ideas or advice, there's often someone with experience who can help.

We don't have a fixed agenda for our meetups. Sometimes, there might be a Show and Tell talk. Generally, people bring along their latest projects. We work on our own things, occasionally collaborate on an idea, and chat about how best to bring our hacks to life.

We're happy to help you to get started, but we don't have a fixed set of beginner projects or education to offer. If you're looking to explore electronics before buying anything, we can point you at [some online emulators and simulators](/useful-tools). If you're a "self-starter" who wants to learn by doing and collaborating with others, HackWimbledon is a great spot to be.

One more thing: we love to help younger makers to learn, but HackWimbledon is a mixed-age meetup. For guidance, we're asking that anyone aged 14-18 has a parent or guardian with them, and that they stay on-site for the duration of the meetup; generally speaking the meetup is less ideal for those under 14. If you have a strong interest in technology, are happy to be supervised by a parent or guardian who has chatted with us to understand the format, and you're willing to learn independently, you're welcome to come along.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
Thanks to the generosity of Wimbletech, we're able to run a free, volunteer-led meetup - we don't charge to come along. We all do this for fun, and out of own own passions and interests.
{{< /callout >}}

Sound good? We'd love to meet you!
