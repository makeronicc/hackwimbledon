If you're looking for a nerdy day out, the UK has some fantastic museums dedicated to the history of computing, electronics, and technology. Here are some of our favourites.

### The Major Collections

*   **[The National Museum of Computing (TNMOC)](https://www.tnmoc.org/)** - Bletchley Park, Milton Keynes
    Home to the world's largest collection of functional historic computers, including the rebuilt Colossus and the WITCH.
*   **[Centre for Computing History](http://www.computinghistory.org.uk/)** - Cambridge
    A massive, hands-on collection of over 800 historic computers and consoles. Features the famous Megaprocessor.
*   **[Science Museum](https://www.sciencemuseum.org.uk/)** - London
    The computing gallery (Information Age) and the Mathematics gallery are must-sees.
*   **[Museum of Computing](https://www.museumofcomputing.org.uk/)** - Swindon
    The first physical museum of its kind in the UK, with a great focus on the 80s home computer boom.

### Specialist & Retro Museums

*   **[This Museum Is (Not) Obsolete](https://this-museum-is-not-obsolete.com/)** - Ramsgate, Kent
    Created by Sam Battle (Look Mum No Computer), this museum focuses on experimental and "obsolete" electronic technology, synthesizers, and more.
*   **[Retro Computer Museum](http://www.retrocomputermuseum.co.uk/)** - Leicester
    A "living archive" where almost everything is playable.
*   **[North West Computer Museum](https://www.nwcomputermuseum.org.uk/)** - Leigh, Greater Manchester
    A newer museum with over 200 exhibits from early mainframes to modern AI.
*   **[The National Videogame Museum](https://thenvm.org/)** - Sheffield
    Dedicated to the history and culture of video games with hundreds of playable titles.

### Other Interesting Sites

*   **[Bletchley Park](https://bletchleypark.org.uk/)** - Bletchley, Milton Keynes
    The historic site of World War II codebreaking.
*   **[Science and Industry Museum](https://www.scienceandindustrymuseum.org.uk/)** - Manchester
    Home to a working replica of the Manchester Baby, the world's first stored-program computer.
*   **[The Micro Museum](http://www.themicromuseum.org/)** - Ramsgate, Kent
    Located near *This Museum Is (Not) Obsolete*, it houses a private collection of vintage home computers.
